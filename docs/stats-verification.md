# Site statistics: verification checklist

Every numeric claim published on bigbinarytech.com, grouped by page. None of
these can be verified from the repositories, but a services business delivers
plenty of work that never produces a repository, so absence of evidence here is
not evidence the work did not happen. Only you can say which are real.

## How to use this

Mark each line:

- **`[x]`** the number is accurate, leave it alone
- **`[~] <correct value>`** the claim is real but the number is wrong
- **`[ ]`** cannot be substantiated, remove it

Anything still unmarked when we next sit down gets removed, because publishing a
number nobody will stand behind is the one option with no upside.

## Read this first: the counts contradict each other

The same claim appears with three different values depending on which page a
visitor lands on:

| Claim | Value | Pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Projects delivered | **200+** | /about-us-2/, /industries/, /solutions-3/ |
| Projects delivered | **60+** | /careers/, /case-studies/, /resources-2/, /e-books-guides/, /pricing/, /digital-transformation-3/ |
| Projects delivered | **50+** | /ai-automation-2/, /odoo-erp/, /web-development/, /custom-software-development/, /enterprise-odoo/ |

A visitor who reads two service pages sees the company claim it has delivered
both 50 and 200 projects. That damages credibility more than a smaller honest
number would, and it is worth fixing even if you decide to keep everything else.

The same applies to client satisfaction, published as **98%** on six pages and
**92%** on /resources-2/, and to industries served, **15+** almost everywhere.

**One number, one value, used everywhere.** Tell me the real figure and I will
make every page agree.

## Highest exposure claims

These are the ones a prospective client, a partner, or a procurement team is
most likely to test. Worth deciding on these first even if the rest waits.

- `200+ Projects delivered` and its 60+ / 50+ variants
- `80+ POS Systems Deployed` on /retail-pos-2/
- `12M+ Global Users` on /enterprise-odoo/
- `98% Client Retention` and `98% Client Satisfaction`
- `Rated 4.8/5 by readers` and `Downloaded by 1,200+ GCC business leaders`
- `Join 2,500+ business leaders` and `25k+ Monthly Readers` on /blogs/
- The full before and after table on /case-studies/, which attributes specific
  outcomes to unnamed clients
- Named client outcomes on /enterprise-odoo/, for example
  `40% Reduction, Manufacturing Co.`

A rating and a subscriber count are checkable by anyone. If the newsletter does
not have 2,500 subscribers, that one is worth removing today.

## What replaces a removed number

Removing a number does not have to weaken the page. What is left is still
persuasive and is all verifiable:

- The technologies actually used, named specifically
- The problem classes solved, described concretely
- The products built and maintained in house
- Registrations and partnerships that are matters of public record: PSEB,
  P@SHA, NAVTTC, Odoo Partner
- Regions served

A page that says "we built a point of sale system that keeps checkout and stock
on the same data" is stronger than one claiming "80+ POS Systems Deployed" that
a client cannot verify.
